The autumn broadsheet went on the press on Thursday, which is the last week of the year we can trust the light. The window above the Vandercook faces west, and by late October the sun drops below the harbour wall a little after four. After that the ink looks right and prints wrong.

## Why the light matters

We mix by eye. A warm grey that reads correctly under the afternoon sun comes out muddy under the shop lamps, and you only find out when the sheets dry. So the rule is simple: no mixing after four, no press runs after half past.

- Mix in the morning, pull a proof, leave it on the sill.
- Come back after lunch and look at it again.
- If it still looks right, print.

The broadsheet itself is four colours this year, which is one more than is sensible. The fourth is a pale ochre that only appears in the masthead, and we argued about it for a week. It stayed.

> A limited palette is not a limitation. It is the thing that makes a sheet look like it came from one hand.

The edition is 180 copies. They ship on Monday.
