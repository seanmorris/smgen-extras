For years I sharpened chisels in a jig because I did not trust my hands. The jig gave perfect edges and took twenty minutes a blade, so the chisels stayed dull, because twenty minutes is a reason not to start.

The trick that worked was not practice, or not only. It was lowering the stakes. A slightly rounded bevel cuts wood perfectly well. Once I believed that, I could hold the chisel at roughly the right angle, lock my wrists, and rock from the shoulders. Thirty seconds a blade. The chisels are sharp now because sharpening is cheap.

```text
coarse  : 10 strokes, until a burr forms along the whole edge
fine    : 10 strokes, lighter, until the burr folds
strop   : 5 strokes on leather, bevel down, then flat
```

The jig is in a drawer. I keep it for the day I grind a new bevel from scratch, which is the one job it is genuinely for.
