A sticking drawer is an argument between two surfaces, and the first job is to find out which two. Rub a soft pencil along the drawer sides and the top edges, push it home, pull it out, and the shiny patches tell you where it binds. Guessing and planing is how drawers end up loose on one side and tight on the other.

Then plane the drawer, never the carcass. The carcass is the fixed thing; the drawer is the thing you can always make again.

Two or three strokes, test, repeat. A drawer that was sticking for a year came right in a quarter of an hour, and the hard part was not the planing, it was deciding to look before reaching for the plane.
