Every walker needs a bad-weather route: short, sheltered, and interesting enough that you do not resent it. Mine goes behind the boatyard, through the lane with the high walls, up the steps past the chapel and back along the terrace where the wind cannot reach.

It is not a beautiful walk. It is a reliable one. In four years I have never been rained on for more than the two minutes it takes to cross the top of the steps.

The thinking that happens on it is a particular kind: not the open, far-looking thinking of the headland, but the close, tidying sort. Problems get sorted into piles. Nothing gets solved, but by the chapel I usually know which pile to pick up first.
