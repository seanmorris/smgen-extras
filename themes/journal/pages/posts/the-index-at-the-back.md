A commonplace book without an index is a drawer. The entries are in there somewhere, in the order they arrived, and after a year that order means nothing.

The index lives on the last six pages. It is not alphabetical, which would mean leaving space I cannot predict. It is by topic, three or four words per entry, with the page number. Reading, making, walking, and a fourth column headed "other" that I try not to let grow.

The habit is five minutes on Sunday: open the week's entries, decide what each one was really about, write one line in the index. The deciding is the useful part. Half the time the entry was about something other than its title.
