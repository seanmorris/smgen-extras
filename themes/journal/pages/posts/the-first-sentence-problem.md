Most of the books I love open with a sentence I would have cut. The opening of a novel carries a weight nothing else in it carries, and writers know it, and the knowing shows. It is the one line written with the reader looking over the shoulder.

> Begin anywhere. The beginning will find you.

That is from a letter, not a manual, and it is the best advice on the subject I have copied out. The sentence that opens the finished book is almost never the sentence that opened the draft. It is the one that survived.

So a bad first sentence buys something: it buys the second sentence, where the writer stops performing and starts telling you something. I have started reading first pages twice, once to get past the opening and once to see what it was protecting.
